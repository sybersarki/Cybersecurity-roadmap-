RISK MATRIX - WORKING NOTES
AITEE Community Health Center Capstone
Analyst: Paul (D3)

How I scored Likelihood and Impact
------------------------------------
Likelihood = how easy is this for an average attacker to pull off right now,
given what the audit already found. Impact = what happens to the org if it works.

1. Phishing + no MFA -> EHR compromise
   Likelihood: HIGH. No phishing training exists and there's no MFA to fall
   back on if a password gets phished. This is the lowest-effort attack path
   available, doesn't need any special skill or tooling.
   Impact: HIGH. Direct path to the full EHR, which is the most sensitive
   system in the whole environment.
   -> Priority: CRITICAL

2. Ransomware via unpatched systems, untested backups
   Likelihood: MEDIUM-HIGH. Patching is irregular so known vulnerabilities
   are sitting open, but this still needs the attacker to find and use an
   actual exploit, slightly more effort than phishing.
   Impact: HIGH, arguably the worst case. Untested backups mean recovery
   isn't guaranteed, so this could mean total operational shutdown, not just
   a data breach.
   -> Priority: CRITICAL

3. Unencrypted shared-drive data exposed (device theft / network access)
   Likelihood: MEDIUM. Requires either physical device loss or a foothold on
   the network first, so it's a second step rather than a direct opener.
   Impact: HIGH. Once someone's in, there's zero protection layer on the
   data itself, no encryption to slow them down.
   -> Priority: HIGH

Why these 3 and not the others
------------------------------------
Insider threat and unauthorized wireless access are real, but they either
need more specific conditions to line up (a disgruntled employee, a rogue
device on-site) or they largely feed into risk #3 once they succeed. Fixing
the top 3 also closes most of the paths the other risks would use anyway.

vendor access, that changes the likelihood scoring on a few of these.
